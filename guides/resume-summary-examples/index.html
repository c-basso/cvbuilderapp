Resume Summary Examples (and How to Write Yours)

Your summary is the first thing a recruiter reads. Three sentences that show who you are, what you’ve achieved and what you want can decide whether they read the rest.

Updated September 30, 20263 min readBy the CV Builder team
Quick answer
  • Formula: [Title] with [X years] in [field] + [best measurable result] + [what you bring / want next].
  • Keep it to 2–4 lines; no “I” or “me”.
  • Rewrite it for each job using words from the ad.
  • Students: use an objective focused on skills and goals.

The 3-part formula

  1. Identity: job title + years + specialty.
  2. Proof: your strongest, measurable achievement.
  3. Value: what you’ll do for the employer.

Examples for experienced professionals

Marketing: Digital marketing manager with 7 years in e-commerce. Scaled paid social revenue from $40k to $310k/month at a 4.2 ROAS. Ready to build and lead a performance team.
Nursing: Registered nurse with 5 years in emergency care and a record of zero medication errors across 2,000+ patient interactions. Calm under pressure, fluent in Spanish.
Software: Backend engineer (6 yrs, Go/PostgreSQL) who cut API latency by 45% and led a migration serving 3M users. Seeking a senior role on a platform team.
Sales: B2B account executive who closed $1.8M ARR in 2025 (128% of quota). Skilled at complex, multi-stakeholder deals in logistics.
Operations: Operations lead with 8 years in warehousing; reduced picking errors by 60% and overtime costs by $90k per year.

Examples for career changers

Teacher → L&D: Former secondary teacher with 9 years designing curricula for 150+ students a year, now moving into corporate learning & development. Certified in instructional design.
Retail → Customer success: Store supervisor who trained 20 staff and lifted customer-satisfaction scores to 4.8/5, transitioning into SaaS customer success.

Examples for students and graduates

Graduate: Economics graduate (First Class) with internship experience in financial analysis and advanced Excel/SQL skills, seeking an analyst role.
First job: Reliable, friendly high-school student with volunteer experience in event organisation, looking for a weekend café role.

Summary mistakes

  • Clichés with no proof: “hard-working team player”.
  • Writing a paragraph of 6+ lines.
  • Talking about what you want from the job only, not what you offer.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good summary for a resume?

Two to four lines that state your title and experience, your strongest measurable result, and what value you bring to the employer.

Should I use a summary or an objective?

Use a summary if you have relevant experience; use an objective if you are a student or changing careers.

How long should a resume summary be?

About 40–80 words, or 2–4 lines.
